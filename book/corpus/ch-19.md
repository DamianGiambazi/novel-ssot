The kitchen. A weekday morning, 7:10. Early spring. The light is less grey.

The kettle. The window. The neighbour's single upstairs light. The figure.

For the first time the protagonist is not in a hurry to leave for work, so they stand there long enough to notice the order of events. Their own kitchen light comes on. Then — always after, never before — the light across the street comes on. The figure has not been standing there by chance. The figure has been standing there when the protagonist is. For years. Watching, or keeping company, or waiting. They cannot know which. They were never unseen.

They switch their kitchen light off, to see what happens. Across the street, after a moment, the light goes off too.
