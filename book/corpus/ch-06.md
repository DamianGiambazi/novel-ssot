Friday. The kitchen at 7:40 with no Sarah. Then the Albion Hotel by the station, 6 p.m. A lobby carpet designed to hide stains.

The protagonist goes to the hotel intending to decline in person — properly, with closure, like settling a claim. They tell themselves this is an administrative act. They take the letter out of the drawer for the first time since Tuesday but still do not read it. The tea box comes out with it. They put the tea on the counter for Sarah, who is not coming.

Forty minutes in the lobby. Daniel comes down. Older, unhurried, fine. The conversation is short and polite. Daniel says he waited on the platform until the last train, then took it. He says it without accusation. He gives them a small, soft, folded thing: the second ticket. He kept it fifteen years. He says, "You look exactly the same. I don't mean that as a compliment." He goes back upstairs.

They have been seen by the one person who knows what happened at 06:52.

On the bus home they find the ticket in their coat pocket and keep their hand around it, as if it might be cold.
