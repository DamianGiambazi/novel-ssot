The kitchen. Tuesday, 7:30, then 7:40. Grey light. The first morning Sarah comes for her lift.

Without reading past line one, the protagonist knows one thing from the envelope's back flap: "The Albion, by the station — until Friday." Daniel is in the city.

The coffee ritual, performed with aggressive precision. The spoon hits the mug harder than it needs to.

7:40. Back door. Sarah, with a box of her own tea. She asks where she can keep it. The protagonist points at the middle dresser drawer, the empty one. She puts it there.

Sarah asks about the weekend. The protagonist makes a joke about her new project. It is too quick and it lands wrong. They hear it land. They regret it immediately and do not take it back. Sarah: "You don't have to do that." She goes out to wait by the car.

Alone for thirty seconds. The envelope comes out from under the fruit bowl and goes into the middle drawer, on top of Sarah's tea. The key comes off the keyring for the first time in nine years. The drawer is locked. The key goes back on the ring.

The drive to work in silence. The radio on. Neither of them hears it. The protagonist does not look out of the window once.

At lunch they find the hotel's website on their phone. They do not call. They clear the browser history, which takes longer than reading the letter would have.
