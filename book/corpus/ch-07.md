The following week. Monday to Wednesday. The office, then the kitchen in the evening.

They call the Albion to "close the matter." Daniel has checked out. They leave a formal message with reception, which they hear themselves dictate like a claims reference.

They draft a reply at work, in a template, with headings. They read it back. It is a settlement offer for a loss nobody has claimed. They delete it.

They ask their manager, in the open-plan, for a transfer to the Leeds office. The manager asks, reasonably, "What's prompted this?" They cannot answer. The pause goes on. The floor goes quiet. Sarah is at the next bank of desks. She does not look up, which is how they know she heard.

Evening. They are holding a cup of coffee they don't remember making. They look across at the neighbour's house. The light is on. The figure is there. And the protagonist realises they are both in the window. They have always both been in the window.

They think: "I wonder if they ever leave that room."

They do not turn away from the window. The light across the street goes off first.
